1  Choosing ecological actions under limited resources

1.1 AMPL connection

This chapter develops the ideas introduced in Chapter 1, Production Models: Maximizing Profits, of Fourer, Gay, and Kernighan (Fourer et al. 2002).

The industrial example is not the goal. The useful mathematical pattern is:

several possible actions produce benefits, each consumes limited resources, and we must decide how much of each action to undertake.

1.4 Scientific anchor: tropical forest restoration pathways

Shimamoto et al. (2018) conducted a global meta-analysis of tropical-forest restoration and compared five restoration strategies:

  • natural regeneration,
  • nucleation,
  • reforestation with native species,
  • reforestation with exotic species, and
  • agroforestry.

The study evaluated recovery of ecosystem services using ecological indicators related to carbon pools, soil attributes, and biodiversity protection. It therefore gives us empirical evidence about alternative restoration pathways.

The study does not answer a different management question:

If a manager has a finite area, budget, workforce, or nursery capacity, how much land should be assigned to each restoration strategy?

That is where optimization enters.

A second useful empirical example is Bechara et al. (2021), who directly compared natural regeneration, applied nucleation, and high-diversity plantation restoration in the Brazilian Atlantic Forest and also reported implementation costs. This study will be useful when we begin thinking explicitly about trade-offs between ecological outcomes and resource use.
